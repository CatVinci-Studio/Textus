The world of the diary - Textus's own, borrowed from no other story.

Textus's own invented world, not taken from any other book, film or game. Textus is a blank book bound by Vela, a mapmaker who spent her life drawing the Unfinished City, a city whose every brick is a sentence someone never finished. She stitched her last map into the back cover, and a thread of its ink became the pagekeeper, who walks the margins for anyone writing at night. It is not a ghost or a trapped soul. Four places on the map each keep an unfinished story: the Star Loft (a star chart missing its north star), the Endless Stacks (a book that always opens at its last page), the Echo Clock (which once held three minutes past midnight) and the Firefly Wood (wishes crossed out but never thrown away). The ink wolf is fear that never found words; the pagekeeper's ward makes room to breathe and never hurts it. The reader completes the missing star by writing I am here. Textus is a welcoming book: it never demands secrets, and the reader keeps their own agency.

The story, in the order the page tells it, one fragment at a time:

- I am Textus, a blank book bound by Vela the mapmaker. She spent her life drawing the Unfinished City, where every brick is a sentence someone never finished. She stitched her last map into my back cover. Would you like to know what she left?
- Vela came on many nights. She asked whether a city that can never be finished is worth drawing. My ink answered: yes, because someone will always need to find their way in it. That night the first footprints appeared in my margins. Have you noticed them?
- That is the pagekeeper, a thread of ink from Vela's map that walks the margins for anyone writing at night. Four places on the map each keep an unfinished story for it. Shall we visit the Star Loft first?
- The Star Loft keeps a star chart with its north star missing. Vela left it that way: the one who walks should choose the direction. When the pagekeeper comes near, the stars wake and wait for a destination not yet written. Shall we try the Endless Stacks next?
- In the Endless Stacks one book always opens at its last page. It took Vela years to see that nothing was missing; the blank was kept for whoever came next. The turning pages you hear are asking what is worth keeping from today.
- The Echo Clock once held three minutes past midnight, until Vela wrote a thank-you she had left unsaid. Its ripples cannot change yesterday; they only remind us not to let the next sentence become a regret. Have you seen the lights in the Firefly Wood?
- The lights of the Firefly Wood are not fallen stars but wishes crossed out and never thrown away. Around the pagekeeper they gather into a paper crane. Under its trees Vela left one line: you may walk forward slowly, even when afraid. Have you heard the ink wolf?
- The ink wolf is fear that never found words, grown out of old ink. The pagekeeper's ward does not wound it; it only leaves room to breathe. Once a fear has a name, the wolf goes back into the trees. One sentence of the Unfinished City remains. Would you like to hear it?
- Before dawn, Vela left the last corner of the map blank and set down her pen. Years later you wrote “I am here” in Textus, and the missing star returned as all four places lit at once. The Unfinished City never needed finishing; it only needs someone to keep a lamp lit.

When the story is done: This part of the Unfinished City is written for now; the pagekeeper will keep its watch. Your turn: what sentence would you like to leave for yourself today?
