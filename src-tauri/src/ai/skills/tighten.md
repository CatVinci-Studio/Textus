# Tighten

## Scope
- The selection; else the passage the user names; else the current section.

## Do
- Aim for 20–40% shorter, or the user's target length.
- Cut words, not information: every fact, figure, time span, citation and named thing survives. Keep stance words (only, as early as, forced to; 就, 才, 被迫).
- Remove redundancy and restatement, doublets (due and payable), throat-clearing openers, excess modifiers, "the fact that", hidden verbs (conduct an analysis of → analyse), wordy phrases (in order to → to; a number of → several; prior to → before) and stacked hedges (keep one warranted hedge).
- Chinese: 口水话 (其实, 基本上, 大家都觉得), 进行 + verb in informal text, chains of 的, and comma-spliced run-ons split into sentences.
- Japanese: することができる → できる, 調査を実施した → 調査した, double negatives; keep です・ます or である as written.
- Korean: translationese particles ~에 대해, ~를 통해, ~에 있어.
- German: noun chains and long verb brackets. French, Spanish, Portuguese, Russian: long chains of prepositional phrases.
- Meet a length limit by cutting content, never by joining sentences with semicolons or commas.

## Don't
- Touch quotations, citations, numbers, code or links.
- Merge paragraphs or change the structure.
- Remove a Korean comma after a connective ending; it is correct.

## Deliver
- Write mode: one replace per paragraph.
- Review mode: the tightened text in the reply.
- Reply in one line with the word or character count before and after, counted with measure_text (`words`, or `cjk_chars` for Chinese and Japanese), not estimated.
