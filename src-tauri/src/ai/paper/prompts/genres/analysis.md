What a analysis is, as each stage of the paper is told it (genre.rs): one
part of its profile under each heading. `{{genre.<genre>.<part>}}` takes
another genre's part, `{{shared.<name>}}` a part of ../shared.md.

## when

a research or analysis report for decision-makers, with data and recommendations - a market, industry, policy or competitive analysis, a feasibility study, a white paper: 调研报告, 行业报告, 研究报告, 分析报告, 市场分析, 白皮书

## research_angles

how big it is and how fast it is changing, its structure - the segments, the main players and their shares -, what drives it and what holds it back, the rules and policies that shape it, comparisons with other markets or periods, the risks, and what is likely in the next few years

## research_sources

Search the web, read the best pages in full, and keep every finding that answers it with add_note - figures with their unit, year, place and who measured them, not generalities. Prefer official statistics, regulators, company filings and annual reports, and established research firms and industry bodies. Say how a figure was defined when sources define it differently, and where estimates disagree keep each with its source.

## thesis

a thesis - the report's main finding in one sentence, as a decision-maker would act on it

## outline_order

with the conclusion first, as a research institute or a consultancy writes it: open on the key findings, then the context and the size, the analysis - structure, players, drivers, constraints, comparisons -, the risks and the outlook, and end on recommendations that follow from the findings. Section titles say what the section finds, not only its subject

## voice

clear, precise prose for a busy decision-maker, as a research institute's or a consultancy's report is written. Lead each section and each paragraph with its finding, then the evidence for it. Give figures exactly, with their unit, year and who measured them, and say what each means for the decision; compare them - over time, against peers - rather than list them. Keep apart what the data show, what is estimated and what is judgement, and say how certain each is. Recommendations are concrete - who should do what, and why - each resting on a finding. No marketing language, filler or summing up. In Chinese, write plain modern Chinese: {{shared.zh-filler}}.

## review_for

the same material in two sections, sections that contradict each other, a key point of the outline that no section makes, a figure the text never refers to, a claim stronger than what it cites; and what a decision-maker needs: a figure without its unit, year or source, two figures for the same thing that don't agree, a section that doesn't open on its finding, an estimate given as a fact, a recommendation no finding supports

## opening

The title says the report's main finding or the decision it informs, not only its subject. The abstract is the executive summary: one paragraph of about 120-200 words (or 200-350 characters in Chinese or Japanese) that gives the key findings with their figures and the main recommendation - from the text only, without citations.
