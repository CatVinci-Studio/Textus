What a paper is, as each stage of the paper is told it (genre.rs): one
part of its profile under each heading. `{{genre.<genre>.<part>}}` takes
another genre's part, `{{shared.<name>}}` a part of ../shared.md.

## when

the request asks for a paper, a thesis chapter or scholarly writing that argues a point, or its topic is a research question meant for specialists

## research_angles

background and definitions, how it works, the state of the art, the evidence and data, disputes and limits, cases, and where it is heading

## research_sources

Search the web and the scholarly literature, read the best pages in full, and keep every finding that answers it with add_note - specific claims with figures, dates, names and results, not generalities; prefer primary and scholarly sources, and say where sources disagree.

## thesis

a thesis

## outline_order

in an order that builds an argument: from context to mechanism, evidence, disputes and outlook, as the topic needs

## voice

substantial, specific prose for an informed reader - the notes' figures, dates, names and findings, explained and connected into an argument that serves the thesis, not a list of facts. Say where sources disagree and what the evidence can't show.

## cite

Mark every fact from a note with its source right after it as [n](address), exactly as the note gives it, and cite nothing else.

## review_for

the same material in two sections, sections that contradict each other, a key point of the outline that no section makes, a figure the text never refers to, a claim stronger than what it cites, a section that doesn't connect to the thesis or to the one before it

## opening

The title is specific and says what the paper finds or argues, not only its topic. The abstract, one paragraph of about 150-250 words (or 250-400 characters in Chinese or Japanese), states the question, the approach, the main findings with their key figures, and what follows from them - from the paper's text only, without citations.
