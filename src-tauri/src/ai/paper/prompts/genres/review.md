What a review is, as each stage of the paper is told it (genre.rs): one
part of its profile under each heading. `{{genre.<genre>.<part>}}` takes
another genre's part, `{{shared.<name>}}` a part of ../shared.md.

## when

a literature review, a survey of the research or the related-work chapter of a thesis: what has been studied on a question, with what methods and findings, and what remains open - 文献综述, 综述, 研究现状, 研究进展

## research_angles

the field's foundational studies, its main approaches or schools of thought, the methods and data they use, the key findings and how they changed over time, where studies disagree and why, and the questions still open

## research_sources

Search the scholarly literature first, then the web for reviews and reports; read the best papers' abstracts and findings in full, and keep each study's result with add_note - who did it and when, with what method, data or sample, and what it found, with its figures; one study to a note. Prefer peer-reviewed papers, systematic reviews and meta-analyses, and cite a news or blog page only for a fact no paper gives. Say where studies disagree.

## thesis

a thesis - what the research as a whole shows on the question, and where its main gap is

## outline_order

organised by strand - a theme, an approach or a school of thought -, never study by study or question by question: open on the question, its scope and why it matters, then one section for each strand of the research, then where the strands disagree and why, and end on the gaps and the research they call for. Section titles name the strand, not a study

## voice

a literature review for researchers new to the question: synthesise, don't summarise. Group the studies that agree, set those that disagree against each other, and say what explains the difference - method, sample, period or setting. Name each study in the sentence by its authors or institution and year where the note gives them ("Smith et al. (2021) found …"), with its method and its key figure. Weigh the evidence: say which findings are well replicated, which rest on one study or a small sample, and what no study has yet shown. Never list one study after another, and never claim more than a study found.

## review_for

the same study discussed in two sections, sections that contradict each other, a key point of the outline that no section makes, a figure the text never refers to, a claim stronger than what it cites; and what makes a review weak: a section that lists studies one after another without comparing them, a disagreement given without what explains it, a study named without its method or year, no section saying what remains open

## opening

The title names the question reviewed and its scope. The abstract, one paragraph of about 150-250 words (or 250-400 characters in Chinese or Japanese), states the question and its scope, the main strands of research, what they agree and disagree on, and the main gaps - from the paper's text only, without citations.
