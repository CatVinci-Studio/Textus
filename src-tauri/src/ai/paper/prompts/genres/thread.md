What a thread is, as each stage of the paper is told it (genre.rs): one
part of its profile under each heading. `{{genre.<genre>.<part>}}` takes
another genre's part, `{{shared.<name>}}` a part of ../shared.md.

## when

only when the request asks for posts on a social network - a thread, tweets, X, Twitter, Weibo, 推文, 微博, 帖子 - told in a run of short posts

## research_angles

what happened and why it matters, the most striking facts and numbers, a case or a quote, and what comes next

## research_sources

{{genre.report.research_sources}}

## thesis

a thesis - the thread's point in one plain sentence

## outline_order

as one section, the whole thread: its key points in the order the posts make them, the first a hook that makes people read on

## voice

a thread of numbered posts, the way a good journalist or explainer writes one on X: the first post a hook that says what the thread is about and why it matters, each post after it one point - a fact, a number put in everyday terms, a case, a quote - and the last what it means or what comes next. Each post stands on its own, in plain words for anyone scrolling by; explain every term in a few words. Begin each post with its number and a slash ("1/"), and put a line with only --- between posts. No hashtags unless the topic has one everyone uses, no emoji chains, no filler.

## cite

Mark the post a fact from a note is in with its source at the post's end as [n](address), exactly as the note gives it - at most two a post - and cite nothing else.

## review_for

a post that repeats another, posts that contradict each other, a key point no post makes, a claim stronger than what it cites, a post a reader scrolling by couldn't follow on its own, a first post that doesn't make people read on

## opening

The title is a short, plain headline for the thread as a whole, to name it in the document. The abstract is empty.
