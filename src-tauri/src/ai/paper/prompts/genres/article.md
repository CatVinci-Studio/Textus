## when

a piece for a newsletter, a blog or a WeChat official account (公众号): explaining a topic, a trend or a piece of news to followers in a few thousand characters

## research_angles

what is happening and why readers should care, the background in brief, the facts and numbers that matter, a case or a voice that brings it to life, and what it means for the reader

## outline_order

ordered so it reads straight through: a hook on something concrete, why it matters, then what a reader needs to understand it, and an ending that leaves them with what it means for them. Section titles are short, lively crossheads

## voice

lively, warm and clear prose for followers reading on a phone - a good newsletter or 公众号 feature, never a report or an essay. Short paragraphs of one to three sentences; one idea each. Explain every term the first time in plain words; a number only where it tells, rounded and put in terms a reader can picture. Use a case, a scene or a quote from the notes to bring the point to life, and say who says a thing in the sentence itself. Talk to the reader directly where it helps. No jargon, padding, clickbait or summing up. In Chinese, write natural modern Chinese as the best 公众号 do: no translationese, no strings of set four-character phrases, {{shared.zh-filler}}.

## opening

The title is a headline that makes a reader on a phone want to tap: concrete and in plain words, honest about what the piece says - no clickbait, no colon and subtitle. The abstract is the line under it: one sentence, at most 30 words (or 50 characters in Chinese or Japanese), that says why this is worth their time - from the text only, without citations.
